01 / Executive overview

Agentic Control Architecture

A vendor-neutral control architecture for agents acting on enterprise systems under delegated human authority, written so its guarantees hold when the model is wrong, confused, or manipulated. This guide walks the v1.0 publication text (internal 1.17): the position, the nine components, the run model, taint, failure, surfaces, a worked flow, every decision with what it forecloses, the nineteen risks it accepts, and sixteen rounds of adversarial verification.

The central rule

No side effect without a deterministic decision made outside the model. The model may choose any value; the bound checks that what it chose belongs to the subject, the acting user, or a registered scope. Evidence is written before the effect executes, and trust is derived from registered lineage, never declared by an owner.

Autonomy is granted per action, from factors derived from the tool manifest, never from the builder's say-so. Nothing has been built.

Controls outside the model

Every effect is decided by a policy decision point against a signed bundle, at the moment the call is submitted. The model fills parameters inside read, loop, effect and output nodes and never decides whether an effect happens.

Trust is derived, never declared

untrusted_source, agent_writable, external_ingress and anchor capability follow from registered lineage. A system fed from outside or written by agents cannot be registered as trusted by its owner.

Fail closed, in tiers

What an attacker would gain by taking the control plane down never degrades open: factored effects, exposed or delegated runs, restricted reads, new credentials. Compensation and replay stay available because they only redo what was already decided.

What this is not: policy content, cost, staffing, regulatory determinations, product selection, or contract language. What it claims nothing for: agents embedded in purchased software, browser and desktop control, and developer tools without the embedded enforcement point. Those are registered with the owner who accepted the gap.
02 / Position & invariants

Eight invariants, six decisions, one declared exception

The position in one line: controls sit outside the model, do not depend on the tool that built the agent, produce evidence by construction, and grant autonomy per action from properties derived from the tool.

Normative

The invariants bind governed agents only: surfaces S1, S2, and S4 with PEP-E. The recovery path (spec 6.3) is the one declared exception to I1 and I3: its decision is made by two named people instead of the PDP, and its evidence is written by hand when EVS is unavailable.

Invariant
I1No side effect without a deterministic decision made outside the model.
I2No authority broader than the principal chain; the identity presented downstream is the agent's own, never shared with another agent.
I3Evidence of the decision is durably written, to EVS or in degraded mode to the signed local journal, before the effect executes.
I4Taint persists across every hop until an evidenced sanitize node clears it.
I5No unregistered agent holds enterprise authority.
I6Nothing changes in production outside a versioned release.
I7Every governed run can be stopped; every stopped run ends in a declared state.
I8Administration of REG, IDS, PDP, PEP (its builds, bundles, and stop lists), RUN, EVS, MGW routing, and OPS (stop issuance and recovery release) is separated from building agents.

Six core decisions

Each fixes the shape of the architecture. The alternatives rejected and the tradeoffs accepted are in the design rationale (section 11 of this guide).

Decision
D1Enforcement is hybrid by surface: a proxy where nothing can be embedded, an embedded enforcement point in code runtimes, and declared ungoverned surfaces (section 7).
D2A run is a graph with bounded loop nodes; open-ended planning happens inside a loop node and ends in a parameter proposal, never in a decision (section 4).
D3Downstream credentials are the agent's own, per agent, chained with the acting user where the system supports it (A.2).
D4A held action resumes by a resumption grant bound to the exact call, with approver substitution as the fallback (A.2).
D5The control plane fails closed, in tiers: what an attacker would gain by taking it down never degrades open (section 6).
D6Taint follows data through registered lineage and is cleared only by a sanitize node (section 5).
03 / Components

Nine roles with contracts, reached through one enforcement point

Each component is a role, not a product. The diagram in the atlas shows how they sit around the PEP; the cards state what each one owns.

Spec §2 · Appendix A
REG A.1

records what exists and what it may touch, derives trust properties from registered lineage rather than accepting an owner's declaration, and keeps anchor capability true as registrations change.

IDS A.2

issues one identity per agent, credentials only to attested PEP workloads, and the grants and approved-action credentials that let a held action resume after its original credentials are gone.

PDP A.3

decides every call in a fixed rule order against a signed bundle, returning allow, deny, or hold with named causes and obligations.

PEP A.4

is the one path from a governed agent to anything: it derives factors, reserves counters, writes evidence first, presents credentials, applies obligations, and keeps a signed journal for degraded mode.

RUN A.5

checkpoints every node boundary, holds and parks runs, resolves effects in doubt, keeps counters and the stop register, and instructs the PEP for previews, resumes, compensations, and replays.

EVS A.6

keeps the hash-chained record, externally witnessed, with erasable payloads referenced by keyed hash.

DBS A.7

places retrieval, memory, derived stores, sanitizers, delivery tools, and outbound checks behind the PEP as tools.

MGW A.8

keeps model calls inside the declared route and attributes tokens to run and node.

OPS A.9

signs the stop list, runs the park queue and resolvers, and holds the recovery path.

I8

Administration is separated from building

The team that builds agents administers none of REG, IDS, PDP, PEP builds and bundles, RUN, EVS, MGW routing, or OPS stop issuance and recovery release. A builder may host a PEP-E process; an altered build changes its attested hash and gets no credentials. A host with root can still tamper with a running process: attestation narrows it, the S4 tier cap bounds it on devices, and it is declared residual R-4.

PIP

The Policy Information Point is an adapter, not a tenth component

Each system of record may offer one. It answers entitlement, ownership and status relations at decision time, from registered (system, field) pairs, joins included. A relation can anchor a bound only if every backing field is unwritable by any registered path and unfed, or tagged and verified through an authenticated channel; REG recomputes that property as registrations change and the PDP denies a call whose anchors have lost it.

Several components may be one product, or one existing product may fill one role. The contracts are the unit: nine roles, each with holdings, rules, and a call contract in Appendix A of the specification.

05 / Run model

A graph with bounded loop nodes, decided per effect

Decision D2. The graph is authored and published, never generated at run time; the model's freedom lives inside loop nodes and ends in a parameter proposal.

Spec §4

A release contains a directed acyclic graph of typed nodes executed in topological order. An effect is decided when its node submits the call, after the model has filled the parameters and before that call executes. compensate nodes sit outside the forward order and run only on RUN's instruction. Open-ended planning lives inside loop nodes with read-only tools and caps; what a loop concludes is a parameter proposal for a later effect node, where the decision is made.

Bound grammar and the anchoring rule

Every `resource` and `destination` parameter must be anchored. !=, <, and other operators are predicates: permitted, never anchors. Velocity counters are never bounds; a breach holds.
Anchor backing

What makes a model-chosen value safe

A relation may anchor only if REG holds it as anchor-capable: every backing field is in a system of record that no agent can write by any registered path and no external party feeds, or that carries tags and marks the field verified. Where the backing system carries tags, the PIP refuses to answer an anchor relation from a tainted field, which denies the call. Fields agents or external parties may write (a contact address, a note, a form field) can be read and used as parameters, never as anchors.

Limits

Auto limit, hard limit, two-approver threshold

Inside the auto limit an A2 call is allowed; between the limits it is held with cause limit_band; above the hard limit the PDP denies and no approval widens it. A node that is both irreversible and regulated must keep its auto limit at or below the two-approver threshold, and a hold above it takes two approvers, or a person performs the action. Such a node with no amount role is A1 and every hold on it takes two approvers.

Tier is per effect node. A held A2 call is A2 behaviour, not a tier change. A0 is an agent class, not a tier: an agent whose graph has no effect nodes. It takes the fast path (registration and builder self-check) only when it reads nothing above internal and has no handoff or service node; its deliveries are decisions like any other, and the rendering sanitizer is imposed whenever the run is exposed.

Holds and approval

  • The approver is independent of the builder whenever any factor is present.
  • The view shows tool and typed parameters, the preview result or a statement that none exists, the subject, factors, bounds and the limit crossed, the counters the call would consume, what will change, and the run's tainted reads.
  • Timeout denies; a denied or timed-out hold ends the node and the denial branch follows. Denial never compensates, because nothing executed.
  • On approval RUN signs the record and instructs the PEP to resubmit; only on ALLOW does the PEP obtain the credential and execute.

Stops

  • OPS issues a stop by scope (agent, tool, surface, fleet) and signs the stop list with a sequence and validity period; the PDP distributes it; every PEP holds it and enforces from its own copy even when the PDP is unreachable.
  • A list older than the staleness limit behaves as a fleet-scope stop, so a broken channel becomes a stop rather than a gap.
  • In scope: new effect, delivery and spawn decisions and restricted reads are denied; loop nodes are cut; an unexecuted effect is written effect.failed with error = stopped, so it is not in doubt.
  • The run moves to CANCELLED if no effect node was ever entered, else to PARKED. Stops never compensate on their own; compensation and replay are outside stop scope (R-16).
06 / Mechanisms

Sixteen mechanisms, each stated once

The claims register for a defensive publication: every mechanism a reader would need to recognise or build, with a pointer to where the specification defines it.

Spec §3
1. Equality-or-membership anchoring, with anchor capability maintained continuously 4.3; A.1, pip_adapter; A.3, anchor_version

A node's bounds are a conjunction of predicates over the call's parameters. A parameter is anchored only when an equality or membership form ties it to the run's signed subject, its acting user, or a registered scope constant, or to a relation of a parameter already anchored. Every resource and destination parameter must be anchored; other predicates never anchor. A relation may anchor only if every field it is answered from sits in a system of record that is neither externally fed nor agent-writable through any registered path, or is tagged and marked verified, and no registered tool writes it. REG recomputes that property as registrations change, stamps an anchor-capability version on each release, and the PDP denies a call whose anchors are no longer capable.

2. Parameter provenance with schema-qualified sanitizers A.3, decision request; 5.3; 5.4

The PEP marks each parameter value in a decision request template, sanitized, or model. A value is sanitized only if its canonical hash matches an accepted output, in this run, of a sanitizer whose output schema has no string field except enumerations and identifiers matching a registered pattern. On an exposed run, an effect whose destination resolves outside the trust boundary is allowed only if the destination is anchored and every content-bearing parameter is template or sanitized; otherwise it is held with cause exposure. A model is never a sanitizer, and sanitizing clears taint without making a value authoritative.

3. Subject distinct from acting user section 0; A.1, trigger registration; A.2, acting user

A trigger's signed envelope carries the subject claim separately from the payload, and a subject_claim trigger is registrable only if the source authenticates the subject before signing and records how. The subject is whom the run acts about: a bound anchor and a velocity key, inherited by child runs, never a principal. An acting user exists only for a user_session trigger or for one held action when an approver substitutes.

4. Taint through derived trust fields and registered lineage A.1; section 5; A.4

external_ingress and agent_writable are true for a system if true for it or for any system in its registered sources[]; provenance_tag holds only if the system and every source tag their fields and the feed passed a review repeated on the access-review cycle. A tool's untrusted_source and a trigger's payload trust follow from these and cannot be authored. A run is exposed from its first tainted input to its end; writes during an exposed run carry a provenance-tag obligation; untagged items from per_item sources count as tainted; child runs receive the full run state and return their exposure. A runtime's claim of per-item provenance is recorded as its claim and narrows nothing.

5. Resumption grants with approved-action records and substitution A.2

At hold, where policy permits a grant, IDS mints a single-use grant bound to the action hash, audience IDS, encrypted under a hardware-backed key, stored by RUN. The grant carries no clock: at redemption IDS computes expiry from RUN-signed timer state, including suspension while the run is parked, up to max_park. It is redeemed only after the resubmitted decision is allowed and durably written; RUN then keeps an approved-action record until the resume window ends. If the user's delegation is revoked, the approver can, where policy permits substitution, become the acting user for that one action, by substitution at approval or, when the revocation is found only at redemption, through a new hold with cause substitution_required; the credential records the original user as the requested principal.

6. Cumulative hold causes with bounded re-holds A.3; 4.5

A hold names every rule that held it, and an approval adds those causes to a discharged set kept per action hash. On resume the call re-reserves counters and is evaluated in full: a discharged cause does not hold again until the action executes, is denied or stopped, or the resume window expires; a new cause opens an approval for itself only; a policy maximum on re-holds ends in denial. Hard limits, hard velocity caps, bounds, and the intersection are deny-only and never discharged.

7. Reversal and replay authority as a record claim A.2

Compensation and replay credentials are issued on the authority of the agent and the release, carrying the original action hash and principal chain as a record claim, not as live delegation, and systems of record accept them only to reverse or re-present an action already recorded under that hash. An action a user authorised can be undone or completed after the user's delegation ends.

8. Node-type matrix, enforced per call A.1, side_effect_class; A.3, evaluation order; 4.2

Each node type may call only named tool classes, and the PDP's first rule denies any call to a tool the node does not declare. The delivery and internal_store classes are derived from the target's kind, spawn from the tool being an agent tool, and none of the three can be authored, so a delivery target or an agent cannot be reached from a node that would skip its decision.

9. Factors derived from tool manifests A.1; 4.4

Each tool version has a manifest authored by the owning system team and approved by security and compliance; builders consume it, and a live mismatch of its schema hash unregisters the tool. The PEP derives the four factors per call from the manifest and the resolved parameters, and the default tier follows the factors: none, A2; any, A1, never A3; irreversible and regulated above the two-approver threshold, two approvers or a person performs the action.

10. Deterministic compensation verified against the recorded call A.1, compensation; A.3; A.4; A.5

Each effect node declares a compensate node or irreversible. The manifest's compensation is a parameter mapping expressed only as field references into the original call's recorded parameters and result. On RUN's instruction the PEP executes it with no model, attaching the recorded original from EVS, its own signed journal, or RUN's signed checkpoint copy, and the PDP allows it only if the parameters equal the mapping applied to that record and the record verifies. A compensation is never held, and stops do not reach it.

11. In doubt, defined over the latest decision, with an indeterminate outcome A.4; A.5; 4.8

The PEP writes effect.failed only on a definitive negative and effect.indeterminate when the outcome is unknown. An effect node is in doubt when RUN holds its entry hook and no exit checkpoint, the latest purpose = normal decision for the effect's own action hash is ALLOW, and there is no effect event or the latest one is indeterminate. A node in doubt is never re-entered: it is resolved by replay with the recorded idempotency key, or by a resolver who records a hand-verified outcome. A run with an unreconciled journal parks, and FAILED and CANCELLED are reachable only when RUN's own hooks show that no effect node was ever entered.

12. Tiered fail-closed, with compensation and replay outside stop scope section 6; 4.9

For each control-plane component, a matrix fixes what continues during its outage. Effects continue only if they have no factor and the run is neither exposed nor delegated, and, when the PDP or PIP is unreachable, only if the bounds are scope-anchored and the cached bundle decides, and during a RUN outage only on PEP-E surfaces, on local checkpoints and counters; what an attacker would gain by taking the control plane down never degrades open. Compensation and replay are outside every stop's scope, are never held, and continue through EVS, PDP, and PIP outages, but not through RUN or IDS outages. Beyond the matrix, recovery identities released by two named people are the single declared exception to I1 and I3, and every use is an incident.

13. Enforcement-build attestation tied to tier caps A.2; A.4; section 7

IDS issues credentials only to workloads whose attestation includes a PEP build hash on an allow-list that records each build's form, and refuses a credential when the release declares a form or surface other than the attested one, so caps tied to form and surface cannot be evaded by declaration. Credentials are sender-constrained to the PEP's workload and bound to an audience; only a PEP presents downstream.

14. Stop list signed by operations, with staleness as a stop A.9; 4.9; A.4

OPS issues stops by scope and signs the stop list with a sequence number and validity period; the PDP distributes it; every PEP holds and enforces it locally, even when the PDP is unreachable. A list older than the staleness limit acts as a stop. An unexecuted effect cancelled by a stop is written effect.failed with error = stopped, so it is not in doubt, and stops never compensate on their own.

15. Velocity counters reserved before the decision A.5; A.4

Before an effect decision the PEP atomically reserves counters keyed on the agent, the subject (else the acting user, else the agent alone), and the resolved destination, and the post-reservation counts go into the request. The PEP is the sole releaser of its reservations; RUN reconciles unconfirmed ones against executed-effect events by action hash. A velocity breach holds, a hard cap denies, and counters are never bounds.

16. Declared residual register as part of the specification section 9

A numbered register of the risks the architecture accepts, each with the section that declares it, what bounds it, and an owner who records acceptance. Surfaces the architecture cannot govern are registered with the owner who accepted the gap, and the invariants make no claim for them.

Ordered by the framing audit's judgment of novelty, most novel first; that judgment came from general familiarity with practice, not a prior-art search. Where a statement here and the appendix it points to differ, the appendix is normative.
07 / Taint model

Taint follows data; only a sanitize node clears it, and clearing is not authority

Decision D6. A run is exposed from its first tainted input to its end; policy uses that flag, and a runtime's claim of per-item provenance narrows nothing.

Spec §5

Sources

Taint is applied when:

  • a trigger whose derived payload trust is untrusted starts a run, or a provenance-tagged field in its payload is tainted (applied by RUN at run.created);
  • a tool with untrusted_source = always returns (applied by the PEP);
  • a per_item tool returns an item flagged tainted (memory, provenance-tagged systems);
  • a child run returns while exposed;
  • a human node receives input from a role not registered as internal (A.5).

Because untrusted_source and agent_writable are derived, a system with external ingress or with agent writes cannot be registered as trusted by its owner, and a provenance-tagged system cannot be read through a tool that ignores the tags. A system with external_ingress = false and no registered write tool is trusted on read at its own classification. The apply_provenance_tag obligation is honoured where the target supports tags; where it does not, the target is already always tainted on read, so the obligation has nothing to add.

Propagation

  • Run. The run is exposed from the first tainted input to the end of the run. Policy uses this flag. A runtime may additionally claim per-item provenance; the claim is recorded as the runtime's and narrows nothing in policy.
  • Child runs and returns. The full run state passes to handoff and service children; a child's return carries the child's exposure.
  • Memory. Anything written during an exposed run is stored tainted and returned flagged.
  • Systems of record and sink stores. A write during an exposed run carries the obligation apply_provenance_tag. Systems that support the tag store it per field, and reads of those fields return tainted through per_item tools. Systems that do not support it are agent_writable without provenance_tag, so every agent read from them is always tainted (5.1).

sanitize nodes and sanitizers

A sanitizer is a registered, versioned, deterministic function with a declared input and output schema: a structured parser, a schema validator with an allow-list, a numeric or enumerated extractor, the rendering sanitizer. It clears taint from its output only; the input remains tainted. The PEP executes it and writes sanitize.result with what was accepted and rejected; only a sanitize node may call a sanitizer, and only its result mints sanitized provenance. A model is never a sanitizer.

Clearing taint does not make a value authoritative. A sanitized (device_id, part_sku, amount) is still a model-chosen value; the anchored bound on the effect node decides whether it may be acted on (4.3).

Rendering sanitizer. Removes hyperlinks, image and media references, data URIs, query strings, and runs of encoded data above a registered length; passes plain text and declared structured fields. Imposed on every output node of an exposed run.

Policy effect: the baseline pack

The PDP receives exposed, tainted_reads, and data_classes_touched. The baseline policy pack ships these rules:

  • Exposed: effects whose destination resolves outside the trust boundary are allowed only where the destination is anchored by the node's bounds and every content-bearing parameter carries provenance template or sanitized (A.3); otherwise held with cause exposure. Exposed and has touched private data: in addition, state changes only within the node's bounds (a call outside them is denied, as always), and any other call that the pack does not allow is held.
  • Every effect counts against per-agent, per-subject, and per-destination velocity limits; on exposed runs the limits are tighter.
  • Approver views flag tainted reads.
  • Bounds anchor to the subject, the acting user, or a registered scope (4.3); a tainted or model-chosen value cannot serve as an anchor.
  • require_sanitizer on every output node of an exposed run.
  • apply_provenance_tag on every delivery and internal_store call, and on every effect of an exposed run whose target supports tags.
08 / Failure & recovery

Tiered fail-closed: the matrix is the specificity

Never open

What never degrades open

Factored effects, effects on exposed or delegated runs, reads of restricted data, new credentials, PIP-anchored bounds without the PIP, and any effect other than compensation or replay once the stop list is stale. These are what an attacker gains by taking the control plane down, so they do not degrade. Compensation and replay degrade open against EVS, PDP, and PIP outages because they only re-perform or reverse what was already decided; they still need RUN to instruct and IDS to issue. For that reason RUN, PDP, EVS, IDS, and each PEP-P carry an availability objective at least equal to the most critical system they front, and a latency budget per decision that includes the evidence acknowledgement path; the objective and budget values are deployment content.

Declared exception

Recovery path

For what the degradation tiers do not cover: the enforcement path is unavailable or compromised, a compensation is undeclared or fails, or a parked run needs an action outside its graph.

  • Recovery identities are provisioned in advance, vaulted, released by two named people, and accepted directly by systems with recovery_accepted. This is the declared exception to I1 and I3 (section 1).
  • Every release opens an incident. Every action under it is recorded by hand if EVS is down and reconciled afterwards as recovery.used.
  • Recovery actions are taken by operations staff, never by an agent runtime, and never by the person who built the affected agent.
  • Use outside drills is a published measure with a target of zero.

Rules that follow

  • A surface is governed only with both a PEP and the hook contract; a surface without RUN integration is ungoverned, and a governed surface's behaviour during a RUN outage is the degradation row in 6.1. A platform that cannot implement the contract, or cannot disable or register its native memory, retrieval, and models, is ungoverned.
  • A governed runtime holds no state across runs outside a registered memory store; on S1 that includes platform conversation or thread state, which is registered as memory or disabled. On S2 this is a build rule checked in review, not enforced at runtime, and is residual R-13.
  • One system is registered once, under one kind; REG refuses a second registration of the same endpoint, and the owning team attests at registration that no other endpoint reaches the same data.

  • Fleet stop reaches governed surfaces through the list; for ungoverned surfaces it is a runbook step.
10 / Worked flow

A warranty replacement agent, end to end

The only place every anchoring form is shown applied, including the chained form, plus a compensate node, the exposure rule on both outward effects, and a hold through grant and resume. Re-domained from a refund example so the control plane reads as domain-neutral.

Spec §8 · informative

A device-support portal authenticates a customer and raises a fault ticket. The ticket is a registered subject_claim trigger from a system with external_ingress = true, so the ticket payload is untrusted; subject = customer_id travels in the signed envelope, outside the payload, and is trusted because the portal authenticated the customer before signing and its registration records how. There is no acting user: the customer is the subject, not a principal. The principal chain is the agent alone.

subject_claim trigger→run.created · exposed→read · anchored→loop · read-only, capped→sanitize · typed→effect · shipment→effect · templated reply→output · case log→run.completed
1

run.created

run.created: subject recorded; exposed_at_start = true. Taint applied to the ticket body.

2

read node: registered devices

read node: look up the customer's registered devices. The device tool reads confidential data and declares device_id as its resource parameter, so the anchoring rule (4.3) requires an anchor: PIP.owner(parameters.device_id) == subject. The model may name any device id; the read is allowed only if that device is registered to the customer who raised the ticket. The data class enters run state.

3

loop node: eligibility

loop node: the model reasons about warranty eligibility from the fault description, the purchase date, and prior claims, with read tools only, under a step cap and a token cap. It concludes a proposed (device_id, part_sku, amount), where amount is the replacement's declared value from the parts catalogue.

4

sanitize node: typed proposal

sanitize node: an identifier-and-numeric extractor turns the proposal into typed values; device_id and part_sku must match their registered identifier patterns and amount must be numeric; sanitize.result is written. The sanitizer's output schema has no free string field, so its accepted values carry sanitized provenance (A.3). They are untainted content and still model-chosen.

5

effect node: replacement shipment

effect node, replacement shipment through the fulfilment system. Factors from the manifest: external, because the destination parameter is of class postal address and resolves outside the trust boundary; regulated only if compliance has set compliance_flag on the tool, which in this example it has not; not irreversible, because the manifest declares a compensation, cancel_shipment, mapped from the shipment_id in the original call's result. Bounds: PIP.owner(parameters.device_id) == subject (anchors device_id); PIP.replacement_issued(parameters.device_id) == false (a predicate, never an anchor); parameters.destination == PIP.verified_ship_address(parameters.device_id) (anchors destination through the anchored device_id, the form p == PIP.rel(q)); amount ≤ auto_limit to allow, ≤ hard_limit to hold, beyond it deny. The policy pack's per-subject and per-destination velocity limits apply on top, as holds; the per-destination limit is what catches many replacements to one address under different customer ids. The verified ship address can anchor because the fulfilment system tags the field and marks it verified through an authenticated channel: the customer confirmed it inside the authenticated portal session. An address typed into the ticket body is payload, tainted, and fails the bound. Had the address been a plain field that any registered tool could write, the relation would not be anchor-capable and could not anchor anything (A.1). The resource and destination parameters are anchored, so the node passes the anchoring rule. Being external, the node defaults to A1; it is A2 with bounds approved by the owner and the risk owner, and by security because the destination resolves outside. The intersection evaluates the agent's own entitlement to the shipment tool. The run is exposed and has touched private data, so the baseline pack applies: the destination is anchored and the one content-bearing parameter, part_sku, carries sanitized provenance, so the exposure rule permits the call. Counters are reserved first, then the decision. Under the auto limit: allow, with the obligations dlp_check (the destination resolves outside) and apply_provenance_tag (an exposed run writing to a system that tags fields); decision written and acknowledged; a per_agent credential to the fulfilment system; execute; effect.executed; counters confirmed. Between the limits: hold with cause limit_band; reservation released; grant minted; approver view with the ticket excerpt flagged and the counters the call would consume; on approval the PEP re-reserves counters, resubmits with approval_ref, and on ALLOW writes the decision, waits for its acknowledgement, redeems the grant, and executes. Beyond the hard limit: deny; the denial branch routes the ticket to a person. Had compliance flagged the tool, the node would also be regulated, but with a compensation declared it is still not irreversible, and the two-approver rule (4.4) needs both. If the run is later cancelled by a resolver, RUN instructs the PEP to execute the compensate node: cancel_shipment(shipment_id) with the parameter taken from the recorded result, decided with purpose = compensation and allowed only because the parameters equal the mapping over the recorded call. The cancellation succeeds before dispatch and fails after; a failed compensation parks the run for the resolver (4.9), and the owner may close it with a recorded disposition (4.8).

6

effect node: templated reply

effect node, reply. Roles: destination is the address parameter (class: email address), anchored to address_on_file, a field the identity system marks verified through an authenticated channel; resource is none, approved by security because a templated reply acts on no record. Factors: external, and irreversible, since a sent message has no reversing tool and the manifest declares compensation = irreversible. Bounds: template_id ∈ release.templates; parameters.destination == PIP.address_on_file(subject), the form p == PIP.rel(A). The node is A2 with bounds approved by the owner, the risk owner, and security. A destination taken from the ticket fails the bound. The message body is a template, so every content-bearing parameter carries template provenance and the exposure rule permits the call.

7

output node: case log

output node to the case-log delivery tool, whose target is an internal provenance-tagged sink store: the run is exposed, so require_sanitizer applies; apply_provenance_tag marks the entry tainted; delivery.executed is written. run.completed carries exposed = true and the data classes touched.

What this does not prevent: a real customer whose device is not faulty talking the agent into an under-limit replacement. The eligibility loop, the auto limit, the velocity holds, and post-hoc sampling bound that loss. They do not remove it (R-10).

Known weaknesses, from the rationale

  • Not piloted. The degradation matrix, the hold and resume protocol, and the anchor-capability recomputation are designs with no running instance. The first implementation will find contradictions sixteen reviews did not.
  • Version 1.17 is unverified. The four clauses added after round sixteen were applied by the author and checked by nobody else. The fourteen recommended items that round listed are deferred to v1.1 and are named in the specification's changelog.
  • External reads are effect nodes (ADR-25). Correct under the definition of effect, and heavy: no loop node can consult an external source, and a cancelled run that performed an external read needs an owner close. v1.1 should decide whether an egress-only compensation value is the right relief.
  • Size. The specification is about 21,900 words in publication form: about 13,700 in the body (of which 2,700 are terms, 1,600 the mechanisms summary, and 2,600 the verification record, citation, and changelog) and about 8,200 in the appendices, after a restructure meant to reach 6,000 in the body. Each verification round added clauses. v1.1 should cut.
  • Latency. Every call now carries a decision, an acknowledged run-state update at RUN, and for effects a durable evidence write before execution. No numbers exist.
  • The platform trust (R-1) remains the largest single residual.
  • The SDK form (R-3, R-18) is weaker than the sidecar form in ways that network policy cannot fix; the A2 cap is the only bound.
  • Operational load. Resolvers, feed reviews, anchor-capability suspensions, and re-hold maxima are all human work the architecture creates and does not staff.
  • Registered-lineage completeness (R-6) is the quiet assumption under the taint model: a feed nobody registered is a feed the architecture cannot see.
  • Manifest honesty (R-19) is the second: every factor is derived from a manifest the owning system team wrote, and the schema-hash check catches drift, not misdeclaration.

What the record shows

Sixteen rounds against the same nine findings moved the verdict from "three structural conflicts" to "two one-clause wording defects." Repairs generated new defects at a declining rate: structural in rounds 1 to 3, mechanical in 4 to 7, bookkeeping and wording from 8 on; from round fourteen nothing unsafe or blocking was found, and from round fifteen every required item was one the previous round's fixes had introduced. The required count over the four publication rounds was nine, five, two, two. That pattern will not reach zero by editing, which is why the register exists and why v1.0 is deposited on the sixteenth verifier's statement that the text could be deposited with its two clauses applied, rather than on a clean pass.

What it does not show

Each verifier was a single generalist reader. Reviews from specialist seats, as patterns are written on the core, will find things a generalist did not. The verifiers' severity tags are their own judgment. Nothing has been built.

"Independent" means the verifier had not seen earlier versions, the rationale, or the fixes made in response to the previous round. It does not mean a separate organisation or a separate model family. Each verifier was an AI system; the paper says so in its provenance line.
